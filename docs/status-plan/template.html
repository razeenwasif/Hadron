Using Flux

How to get around Flux and get things done, one area at a time. It was written for Velvet Paper and Classic. Liquid Silk, now the default design, keeps Velvet Paper's layout and modes, adds Agent and Terminal as sidebar modes (the agent and terminal open there, not in a column), and draws its home shortcuts as white line icons; where Classic works differently, the text says so. Keys are written the Mac way. On Windows and Linux, press Ctrl where you see ⌘ and Alt where you see ⌥. These are the defaults: Settings → Keyboard changes any shortcut that isn't fixed, and the menu bar shows the keys you have.

Start here

  1. Open a page

    ⌘T opens a tab. Type an address or a search into the field near the top of the tabs pane and press ↩. ⌘L takes you back to that field.

  2. Find anything, run anything

    ⌘K opens the launcher: one field for the web, your open tabs, history, notes and about 80 commands. > lists the commands.

  3. Switch the tab bar

    ⌥1 to ⌥5 turn the tabs pane into Tabs, Calendar, Mail, Notes or Context. ⌥1 brings the tab list back.

  4. Ask the agent

    ⌘⇧A opens the agent column. It answers from the page you're on, all your open tabs or your notes.

  5. Make room

    ⌘B folds the tabs pane into a strip of icons. ⌘⇧F hides everything but the page; Esc brings it all back.

  6. Bring your browsing

    A first launch opens a six-step welcome: your name, an import from your other browsers, sites for the new tab, the launcher and the agent. Welcome to Flux in the launcher opens it again.

  7. Find a key

    Every command is in the menu bar with its key: File, Edit, View, History, Bookmarks, Go and Window. Settings → Keyboard changes any of them.

  8. Pick a design

    Settings → Appearance → Design switches between Velvet Paper and Classic straight away. The Theme row there colours Classic only.

Tabs and workspaces

Open and close
  • ⌘T opens a browser tab. The ▾ next to + New tab also offers a private tab, a terminal tab, a files tab and any containers you have.
  • ⌘W closes the tab. ⌘⇧T reopens closed tabs one at a time, up to the last 25.
  • ⌘1–⌘8 go to that tab in this workspace, pinned tabs first. ⌘9 goes to the last one.
  • ⌃Tab and ⌃⇧Tab step to the next and previous tab. (⌘Tab belongs to the Mac's app switcher.)
  • Close all tabs in this workspace and Close every tab are in the launcher. Both ask first.
Organise
  • Right-click a tab for Pin tab, Rename tab, New group with tab, Add to group, Split with current tab, Move to folder, Send to workspace and Close tab.
  • Double-click a tab, group, folder or workspace name to rename it. ↩ saves, Esc cancels.
  • Drag tabs to reorder them.
  • A folder parks tabs out of the way and puts them to sleep to save memory.
  • Group tabs by topic appears above the list when Flux has found tabs that belong together.
  • Background tabs sleep in steps. After 5 idle minutes a tab pauses: its scripts, timers and media stop, but it comes back at once. Beyond the four most recent background tabs, or past 256 MB, it unloads; it then shows a moon and "asleep 2 h", and comes back from a snapshot of where you left it, scrolled where you were.
  • A tab playing sound or using the camera, mic or screen never sleeps. One with typing you haven't sent pauses but stays loaded, unless the Mac is critically short of memory. Settings → Memory sets the rest; Sleep background tabs in the launcher unloads them now.
Workspaces
  • Velvet Paper Workspace chips sit above the pinned tabs. Click one to switch, + makes a new one, and right-click opens the workspace panel to rename, recolour or delete.
  • The last button in the footer opens the same panel in both designs.
  • New workspace and Rename workspace are also in the launcher.
  • Each workspace keeps its own tabs; ⌘1–⌘9 count only the one you're in.

Address and search

The address field
  • Type an address or some words and press ↩. Words are searched with your default engine: DuckDuckGo, until you change it in Settings → Search.
  • Start with g, b or ddg and a space to search Google, Bing or DuckDuckGo just this once. !g works too.
  • ↑ ↓ move through the suggestions, ↩ opens one, Esc puts the page's address back.
  • ⌥↩ asks your Omni index for an answer instead of searching.
  • When a page is zoomed, its zoom level shows in the field. Click it, or press ⌘0, to reset.
  • Classic The field always shows the full address; the star and Reader are in the row below it.
The launcher
  • ⌘K (or ⌘⇧K) opens it over the page. One list, always in this order: Search (the web, an address, your engine's suggestions), Tabs, History and bookmarks, Notes from the Indexer, then Commands. Results that arrive late join their own section; the row you're on stays put.
  • @tabs, @history, @notes and > narrow it to one section. With nothing typed it shows your recent tabs; hold ⌘ and press 1–4 to open one.
  • ↩ opens, ⌘↩ opens in a background tab and leaves the launcher up, ⇧↩ opens beside the current page. ⌘K on a row shows its actions: open in background, copy link, split, close the tab, pin to the pages bar, add to Tasks.
  • ⌥↩ adds what you typed to Tasks. A sum like 80 + 15% is answered in the list; ↩ copies the answer.
Find
  • ⌘F finds in the page: ↩ for the next match, ⇧↩ for the previous one, Esc to stop. Velvet Paper The find field takes the address field's place while you search.
  • Semantic find in the launcher searches by meaning across your open tabs.
Shields
  • The shield in the address field counts what Flux blocked on this page, live as it loads. Click it for the domains it blocked.
  • Switch Shields off for the site when one breaks, or off everywhere; the page reloads to show the difference.
  • Hide an element on this page…: click the part to hide (↑ takes its container, Esc cancels). It stays hidden on that site; the popover offers to show hidden ones again.
  • On a Mac, HTTPS-only, tracking levels and Lean mode aren't available yet; they work on Windows.

Tab bar modes Velvet Paper

The switch at the top of the tabs pane changes what the pane holds. Leaving Tabs folds your tabs into a column of favicons down the pane's side, so you can still switch tabs. Click a mode or press ⌥1–⌥5; each mode keeps its place when you come back. Classic has no modes: its footer has a Calendar button and a button for the calendar + mail column instead.

Calendar · ⌥2
  • The month, with ‹ › to move and the teaching week number.
  • The selected day's events with a line at the current time, then tasks that are due, overdue ones marked.
  • The add line takes plain text: lab tue 3pm, dentist tomorrow 9:30am. See the cheatsheet for the rules.
  • Events from a subscribed calendar are read-only (🔒). Subscribe to an .ics feed from the calendar on the new tab page (＋ Calendar).
  • Open the calendar over the page shows the full calendar; ⇥ Column opens the calendar + mail column beside the page.
Mail · ⌥3
  • Your Gmail inbox over IMAP: sign in with your address and an app password, not a Google login. The password goes into the system keychain.
  • Read-only. Looking here never marks mail as seen.
  • Unread or All; click a message for its date and Open in Gmail, which opens that exact message.
  • The one exception: ✓ all marks everything in the inbox as read, and that does apply in Gmail. There's also Refresh and Forget this account.
  • It shows the newest 20 messages in the inbox.
Notes · ⌥4
  • Notes: Markdown notes, one file each, in a "Flux Notes" folder in your Onyx vault (or Flux's own data folder if there's no vault). The Indexer and any Markdown editor can read them.
  • A note opens for reading, with its tasks tickable. ✎ edits the Markdown and saves as you type; the bin moves the note to the Trash.
  • To-do: Flux's task list, the same one Tasks, the calendar and the new tab's Tasks card show. A line is read like the Tasks page (below).
Tasks · flux://tasks
  • Type a task in one line: Submit the lab report friday 5pm !1 #uni ~45m. !1–!3 is the priority, #list the list and ~45m how long, anywhere in the line.
  • A date is read only at the end: "Call Anna tomorrow at 3" gets one, "Meet the Friday team" keeps its words. Dates are day first (1/11 is 1 November); "at 3" is 3 pm, "at 9" 9 am.
  • What was read is highlighted and shown under the field; Esc keeps your words as typed, ↩ adds.
  • List groups tasks Overdue, Today, Tomorrow, Upcoming and Anytime, flagged first; Board has a column per list, and a card dragged across moves list.
  • Completing one offers Undo for five seconds, or ⌘Z.
Time & Focus · flux://time
  • Where the day went: today by the hour, or This week by the day, and your top sites against yesterday (or last week): ↑, ↓, or "About the same" within five minutes.
  • Counted only while Flux is in front, never in private tabs, and kept on this Mac for two weeks. Forget it clears it.
  • Focus: pick 25, 50 or 90 minutes and the sites to close (YouTube, Reddit, X, Instagram, TikTok and Facebook to start; ✕ drops one, the field adds one), then Start focus. The sites you chose are kept for next time.
  • A closed site doesn't load. Its tab shows the time left, Allow 5 minutes and End focus; sent there by a link, Stay on this page keeps you where you were. Subdomains count: closing youtube.com closes m.youtube.com.
  • Pause is a break and doesn't count. The new tab's clock says "Focus · 17:56 left", and Velvet Paper's Focus card starts, pauses and ends the same session.
  • Under the new tab's clock: requests blocked today, time focused against your daily goal (2h; change it on flux://time) and tasks due today.
Context · ⌥5
  • The connections column, inside the pane: Ask your notes, notes related to the page, the system monitor and the Trail.
  • They live in one place at a time. While the column beside the page is open, Context mode says so and offers Bring them here; from the pane, ⇥ Column sends them back.

Split view

Tile up to four
  • Put up to four tabs side by side: web pages, Flux's own pages, terminals or file browsers.
  • Start from the split button in the page actions row, Split view in the launcher, Split with current tab in a tab's menu, or ⇧↩ on a launcher row.
  • In the picker, click tabs to add or remove them, or search tabs, sites and groups and press ↩ to add the top match.
  • Drag the seams between panes to resize them.
  • Exit split is in the picker, the launcher and the tab menu.
Layouts
  • Two panes: Columns or Rows.
  • Three: Main left, Main right, Main top, Main bottom, Columns or Rows.
  • Four: Quad, plus the same six.
  • The layout buttons appear in the picker once two panes are chosen.

The agent

Ask
  • ⌘⇧A opens it. Choose what it reads: This page, All tabs in this workspace, My notes (Onyx, Scroll, Council, your books and pages you've visited, with citations), or Page thread, the page's saved conversation, when it has one.
  • The chips under the scope run common prompts, starting with Summarize.
  • "What can you do" or /help lists everything it can do.
  • Fairy is the default: the panel opens on her and answers come from her models. Voice: the mic button turns on always-listening "Hey Gemma"; hold the push-to-talk button to speak once. Talk over it or press Stop to interrupt.
Act
  • Running a terminal command, editing a file or writing a note always stops at an approval card. rm and other destructive commands are blocked.
  • Reading needs no approval: read src/foo.rs, list ~/Downloads, "read the terminal", "read my editor".
  • /act does one step on the page, /task works through a goal (Tasks on a page), /fix runs, reads the failure and tries again. The full list is in the cheatsheet.
  • Chain requests with "then" or "+": "read src/foo.rs then fix the bug then run the tests".
  • Calendar in plain words: "what's on friday", "schedule lunch with Sam tomorrow at noon". It can change only events made in Flux.
Answers about the page
  • In This page, answers cite the page: click a number and the page scrolls to the passage, highlighted and numbered.
  • A comparison comes as a table. Each claim is labelled Supported (on the page), Inferred (follows from it), Unverified (not on it) or Contradicted; Flux checks the labels and only ever lowers them. Click a heading to sort; Copy CSV copies it.
  • Under an answer: Make tasks proposes tasks you tick to add, and Save as note keeps the answer and its quoted sources in Flux Notes.
  • /restyle larger text, hide the sidebar shows the change on the page at once. Apply saves it as a Boost for the site; Discard puts the page back.
Tasks on a page
  • /task <goal> works on the page a step at a time. Reading, following links and filling in fields run on their own.
  • A step that sends, posts, submits or books stops at a card saying what it will do: Do it, Skip or Stop. It runs once, and only if what it clicks hasn't changed since you said yes.
  • A step counts once the page shows it. Otherwise the feed says "applied, unverified", and the end reads "Finished, but not confirmed" with why.
  • A sign-in or "are you human" page is handed to you: I'm done, Skip this page, Keep waiting (it watches the page for 90 seconds) or Stop. Stop in the task bar ends a task between steps.
  • It never types into password, card or one-time-code fields, never acts on banking, password-manager, health or tax sites, and never clicks place order, pay now, delete and the like: those are yours to do.
Form help
  • Settings → Form help, off by default. Click or Tab into an empty field and your model suggests its text.
  • A short field shows it greyed out: Tab puts it in. A long one (a text box, a rich-text editor) has it copied: ⌘V pastes it.
  • Esc or typing dismisses it. ⌃Space asks now, or for a different answer while one shows.
  • Only the model on this Mac answers. Never on password, card or code fields, sign-in forms, or banking, password-manager, health and tax sites; personal details are skipped unless you turn that off.
  • It applies to pages you open or reload after turning it on. A form inside an embedded frame isn't covered.
Who answers
  • Fairy, your assistant app, answers in the agent panel. She routes each turn to Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity or DeepSeek, and acts in Flux through its tools, with the same approval cards.
  • Stop ends her answer part way, on both sides.
  • Ollama on this Mac (gemma4:12b-it-qat) still does the local-only work: form help, Sentinel's checks, topic groups and semantic find. The Indexer's embeddings always stay on this Mac.
  • The Model server, Gemini key and cloud switch were removed when Fairy became the agent.

Notes and the Indexer

Ask your notes
  • The Indexer answers a question with what you already wrote in your Onyx notes, typed or spoken.
  • Velvet Paper Ask your notes at the top of the connections column (or in Context mode), or Ask your notes (Indexer) in the launcher, opens a sheet over the page: the question, a Tuning card with every match and a minimum-match slider, the answer and its sources. Reindex is in its header.
  • Classic A floating orb you can drag opens the panel beside it; Show or Hide the Indexer orb is in the launcher. Velvet Paper has no orb.
  • Notes also show in the launcher: type and they appear under Notes, or start with @notes.
Keep and connect
  • As you read, the connections column lists your own notes related to the page, and "Seen before" when the page shows an error you've hit before.
  • ⌘⇧O saves the page to Omni. Save for offline keeps a copy in Saved pages.
  • Watch for changes (page actions) keeps an eye on a page; Watched pages in the launcher lists them.
  • Note for this page, in the footer, keeps a note with the page.
  • The Trail is your browsing as a graph; Notebook asks your whole knowledge base.

Bookmarks and import

Bookmarks
  • ⌘D bookmarks the page, or removes the bookmark.
  • Velvet Paper The bookmark bar is a row in the tabs pane, under the pinned tabs. Classic It docks under the page card. Settings → Appearance → Bookmark bar, or Show bookmark bar in the launcher, turns it on.
  • Click a bookmark to open it here, double-click to rename it, × removes it. Right-click for Open in new tab, Open in new background tab, Peek and Copy link.
  • Folders are chips with ▾. Click one for a menu of what's inside.
  • The Bookmarks page (flux://bookmarks) searches them, groups them by folder, and opens a whole folder as a tab group.
Import
  • Import from another browser (launcher, File menu, or Settings → Data → Import…) lists Chrome, Arc, Safari, Firefox, Brave and Zen, plus Edge and Chromium when they're here, each with its profiles and counts.
  • It brings bookmarks (each browser's bookmarks bar becomes Flux's bar, the rest goes under the browser's name), the last 180 days of history, and Arc's pinned sites and Favorites, under Arc/<space>. The other browser can stay open.
  • macOS keeps some browsers' data from other apps (Safari's always; Chrome's and Firefox's on recent macOS). Those show as locked: Open Full Disk Access, turn Flux on, then Check again.
  • Importing again only adds what's new. The Bookmarks page's Import and Import bookmarks from Zen still bring bookmarks alone.

Terminal, editor and files

Terminal
  • ⌘` shows the terminal column. Terminal tabs come from the ▾ next to + New tab, and open with the agent beside them.
  • While a terminal has focus, ⌃ and ⌥ keys go to the shell (⌃R, ⌃W, tmux's ⌃B, ⌥← by a word); ⌘ shortcuts and ⌃Tab are Flux's. On Windows and Linux, where shortcuts use Ctrl, only Ctrl+`, Ctrl+Shift+A, Ctrl+Shift+E, Ctrl+Shift+R and tab switching reach Flux.
  • ⌘⇧R searches your shell history by meaning.
  • Settings → Terminal → Keep sessions across restarts.
Editor
  • ⌘⇧E opens nvim beside the page. Drag the seam to share the row.
  • The agent reads its live buffer, unsaved changes included ("read my editor"), and your selection ("explain this").
Files and PDFs
  • A files tab browses folders with the keyboard (keys in the cheatsheet). File explorer in the footer opens one over the page; Esc closes it.
  • Delete moves to the Trash after asking; with ⇧ it deletes for good, also after asking. ⌘Z undoes.
  • PDFs open in Flux's viewer: mark up, reorder, rotate, delete or extract pages, merge another PDF, and save the result to Downloads. ⌘= ⌘- ⌘0 zoom the document.

Flux pages

Flux's own pages open in a tab like any site. They're commands in the launcher, with your terminal apps: ⌕ in the nav row opens it on that list (or type > and a name). ↩ opens one, and a terminal app opens in a pane; ⌘↩ gives it a whole tab. Pin to the pages bar, in a row's actions (⌘K), puts it in the pages bar. Edit terminal apps… sets which commands are listed.

Settings worth knowing

Settings has ten sections: Appearance, Search, Browsing data, Keyboard, Terminal, Privacy & security, Navigation, Memory, Data and Integrations. The gear in the footer opens quick settings with the everyday switches, without leaving the page.

Look and feel
  • Design: Velvet Paper or Classic, applied at once. Theme colours Classic only.
  • Dark mode (websites) asks sites to render dark.
  • Switches for the bookmark bar, editor column, pages bar, the liquid new-tab background and a frosted window.
Browsing
  • Navigation: Vim link hints and mouse gestures, both off until you turn them on. Keys in the cheatsheet.
  • Memory: sleeping tabs (pause after 5 minutes, unload beyond the four most recent or past 256 MB), Unload after for any tab idle that long, more under memory pressure, and auto-archive stale tabs.
  • Privacy & security: Shields (also from its popover in the address field), HTTPS-only, a proxy, tracking prevention, camera, mic and location blocking, per-site permissions and cookies.
Keyboard
  • Every command in one list, with a search (/). Click a shortcut and press the new keys.
  • A key another command has asks first: Use here moves it. Keys macOS owns (⌘Q, ⌘C…) and the fixed ones (⌘W, ⌘[ ⌘], ⌥⌘I) stay.
  • Esc cancels, ⌫ takes a shortcut off, ↺ puts one back, and Reset to defaults puts them all back.
  • The menu bar, the launcher, tooltips and every open page follow at once. Keys without ⌘ or ⌃ never fire inside a text field.
Integrations
  • Agent file access: the folders the agent may read.
  • Hey Gemma and its voice, and the agent's memory.
  • Form help: when it suggests, personal details, the form's other answers, how answers should sound, and sites it's off on.

Architecture · as of 13 Nov 2026

How Flux is built

A Tauri v2 app: a Rust core that owns every page view, state and service, and a SolidJS shell that draws the interface around the pages. The pages are the system's own web views (WKWebView on macOS and iOS, WebView2 on Windows, WebKitGTK on Linux), one per tab, so Flux ships no browser engine of its own. The decisions behind it are the 21 ADRs in docs/adr/.

Layers
Rust crates · crates/
  • flux-core: the app itself. About 150 modules: tab web views and their layout, session and persistence, shields, sleeping tabs, the terminal's PTYs and nvim, mail over IMAP, calendar, the Indexer, sync, the vault's commands, Sentinel, voice (TTS, STT, wake word), the key registry and the updater.
  • flux-agent: turns a goal into structured DOM actions, labels steps Read, Draft or Commit, and verifies each one against a fresh look at the page.
  • flux-filter: the ad and tracker matching engine behind the request-interception layer; on macOS it compiles to a WKContentRuleList.
  • flux-embed: small local embeddings and greedy clustering for "group tabs by topic".
  • flux-search: engine templates, bangs and search-or-address resolution.
  • flux-vault: the password vault. AES-256-GCM at rest, key in the OS keychain, Argon2 for the master password, importers such as Proton Pass.
  • flux-import: bookmarks, 180 days of history and Arc's pinned sites from Chrome, Arc, Safari, Firefox, Brave, Zen, Edge and Chromium, read from copies so the other browser can stay open.
  • tauri-plugin-flux-webview: native per-tab web views on iOS and Android (ADR 0012, 0021).
The shell · apps/shell
  • SolidJS with Vite, about 280 source files. Fine-grained signals, so a streamed token or a tab title repaints only its own node.
  • Typed IPC: Rust commands export TypeScript bindings (bindings.gen.ts); the shell never strings a command name by hand.
  • Boot loads only what paints the first frame. The launcher, agent panel, terminal, PDF viewer, Flux pages, notch mode and every banner arrive as lazy chunks on first use.
  • Two designs share one component tree: Liquid Silk (the default, with the wave background and drawn white icons) and Classic. Tokens live on :root; design.ts switches them at once.
  • The shell lays out columns and tells Rust where each page view goes (paneGeometry.ts), because native web views sit above the DOM, not inside it.
The agent · Fairy first
  • Fairy, the user's own agent harness (the Lyra repo), is the default and only chat agent. Flux streams to it over flux.sock in Lyra's app-support folder; Fairy routes the turn to Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity or DeepSeek.
  • Fairy acts on Flux through a second socket, fairy-tools.sock in Flux's data folder. Each ask gets a one-time token; tools read pages, tabs and notes freely, and anything that writes or runs goes to Flux's approval card. fairyTools.ts answers in the shell, flux-tools.mjs calls from Lyra.
  • Stop cancels the turn on both sides. Replies are spoken through Fairy's voice, on the Mac or the phone.
  • The Antigravity and Ollama chat providers were removed. Ollama (gemma4:12b-it-qat) stays for background work: form help, topic grouping, semantic find and checks that should never leave the Mac.
  • flux-agent still drives /act and /task on pages: code decides which steps commit, page text is fenced, and password, card and banking pages are off limits.
Pages, privacy and memory
  • Each tab is a native web view the core creates, positions and hides. Containers and private tabs get their own data stores.
  • Shields: flux-filter lists plus cosmetic rules and a per-site element picker; counts per page. Sentinel adds phishing, OAuth and sensitive-site checks.
  • Sleeping tabs in steps: pause after 5 idle minutes, unload beyond the four most recent or past 256 MB, never with sound, capture or unsent typing. Waking restores from a snapshot.
  • The Indexer keeps a local vector store over Onyx, Scroll, Council, your books and visited pages; embeddings never leave the Mac.
Window · notch mode
  • ⌥⌘F makes the window borderless and the size of the whole screen, camera band included, and hides the menu bar and Dock while Flux is in front (notch.rs).
  • The notch's position comes from NSScreen's safeAreaInsets and the free areas either side of it. The shell (notchMode.ts) starts the column under the notch below it, runs the others to the top, and puts the tab's title and the time either side.
  • Settings → Appearance → Keep the menu bar in full screen pins the menu bar instead: the window fills the screen below it.
Budgets, CI and release
  • scripts/perf-budget.mjs fails CI past: 245 KB of boot JS, 47 KB gzipped chrome JS, 73 KB desktop preload, 31 KB entry CSS, a 28 MB binary. Each raise is approved and noted in the script.
  • ADR 0001's targets: first paint under 300 ms, shell interactive within 50 ms of the web view, idle RAM under 150 MB without pages or the model.
  • CI runs Rust and shell tests on Linux, macOS and Windows. scripts/release.sh signs builds for the updater channel; install-macos.sh installs locally.
  • Mobile: iOS first (ADR 0021), through the same shell with MobileChrome.tsx and the web-view plugin.

ReleaseReleasing and updatesrelease.sh, the updater key, what gets checked 
scripts/release.sh 0.2.0 "What changed"   # from a clean main
  • Bumps the version, builds, signs with the Apple Development certificate, packs and signs the update with ~/.tauri/flux-updater.key, commits and tags v0.2.0, and publishes the GitHub release with its latest.json.
  • Installed Macs check a minute after launch and every six hours, download in the background, and offer Restart now or Later. Only a build signed by the key whose public half is in tauri.conf.json, naming the version offered, installs.
  • The updater key is never committed, and has an iCloud backup. Without it, installed copies can't update. Builds aren't notarized, so they're for your own Macs. Windows and Linux don't self-update yet.
  • Debug builds only check when FLUX_UPDATE_URL points at a latest.json.

TroubleshootingTroubleshootingLogs, renderer crashes, local network, browsing data
  • Read the log first. macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/dev.flux.browser/flux.log (and the system log); Windows: %LOCALAPPDATA%\dev.flux.browser\flux.log, the only place release output appears.
  • A page crashes (Windows STATUS_ACCESS_VIOLATION). In order: FLUX_PAGE_SCRIPTS=none and bisect the list; compare with Edge (same engine); try a private tab; FLUX_WEBVIEW2_ARGS=--disable-gpu; FLUX_NO_QUIC=1 and Shields off for the site; copy a Crashpad dump from EBWebView\Crashpad\reports before it uploads.
  • "No route to host" on the LAN with Local Network on (macOS). Stale LaunchServices records hold an old binary's UUID; re-register the installed app so macOS asks again.
  • Browsing data has grown. Settings → Browsing data → Measure shows each store and clears what you pick on the next launch. Nothing evicts them on a schedule.
  • Never test against your real profile. Use a separate data dir; ~/Library/Application Support/dev.flux.browser is the live one.
IntegrationsFairy (Lyra) and other companionsSockets, tokens, where each side lives
  • Chat: Flux connects to ~/Library/Application Support/lyra-companion/lyra/flux.sock (fairy.rs). Lyra's side is flux-link.mjs and chat-link.mjs; a turn can be stopped from either end.
  • Tools: Flux serves fairy-tools.sock in its own data dir. Each ask carries a one-time token; fairyTools.ts answers in the shell, flux-tools.mjs and flux-agent.mjs call from Lyra. Writes and runs go to Flux's approval card.
  • Working on Lyra: use git worktrees, never the main checkout, and coordinate Fairy reinstalls with whoever is working on Lyra.
  • Ollama on localhost:11434 stays for local-only work: Sentinel, form help, topic groups, semantic find.
  • Shell integration for the terminal (prompt marks, cwd) is in docs/shell-integration.md.
ADRsArchitecture decisions21 records in docs/adr, one line each
  1. 0001 · Rust + Tauri v2, not Electron or a Chromium fork. Rust does the heavy work on native threads; SolidJS draws the interface; pages are per-tab system web views. Sets the performance budgets.
  2. 0002 · Arc-style vertical shell. No top tab strip: the sidebar owns navigation, address, pinned grid, tab list and tools.
  3. 0003 · Terminal: real PTY + xterm.js. portable-pty in Rust, streamed over a Tauri channel, WebGL renderer.
  4. 0004 · DOM capture through an inlined plugin. Remote pages may call only dom_publish, granted by a scoped capability.
  5. 0005 · Agent backend: Ollama over embedded llama.cpp. Local HTTP, JSON mode, gemma4:12b-it-qat by default; the inference trait stayed the same.
  6. 0006 · Files tab. A filesystem explorer in the content card, no web view, virtualized and off the main thread.
  7. 0007 · Request interception and filtering. A portable matching engine (flux-filter) plus a thin native interceptor per backend.
  8. 0008 · Mini-extensions. Manifest-declared, capability-gated; the powerful API lives in a Rust broker, content scripts are untrusted.
  9. 0009 · Password vault and autofill. Local-first flux-vault, never synced by default.
  10. 0010 · Knowledge base. A native, cited KB over your own corpora instead of Omni or MCP; reuses flux-embed.
  11. 0011 · Provenance spine (the Trail). A Visit store; browsing becomes a KB source, and graph features are read models over it.
  12. 0012 · Mobile via Termux first, native Android later. Rungs A and B shipped; the native APK boots.
  13. 0013 · Sentinel. Local phishing guard and hardened agency; fails safe to deterministic checks when the model is down. M1–M5 shipped.
  14. 0014 · Scribe. Retired 30 Oct 2026: Scribe is its own app now; Flux's copy and the whiteboard were removed.
  15. 0015 · Theming. Five base tones and six role-named RGB channels, one definition.
  16. 0016 · Agent write access. A closed vocabulary of note actions that can't express a destructive edit.
  17. 0017 · Sync. Through a folder you already sync (iCloud, Syncthing…); the vault deliberately doesn't travel.
  18. 0018 · Cloud escalation (Gemini). Superseded: removed when Fairy became the agent.
  19. 0019 · Retire flux-term. xterm.js stays; ligatures and inline images added on it.
  20. 0020 · Gemini through Antigravity. Superseded: removed with the other providers; Fairy reaches Antigravity itself.
  21. 0021 · iOS first. The Swift half of the web-view plugin and a phone-first interface, shared with Android.

Fairy as the agent has no ADR yet; the changelog's Unreleased section records the change.
